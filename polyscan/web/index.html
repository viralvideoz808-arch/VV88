How the strategy works

  1. One number settles everything. Each window resolves Up if Chainlink's 60-second average price at the end is ≥ its value at the start. The previous window's final price is the next window's price to beat, and every window ending at the same moment settles on the same final number (checked on 2,065 resolved windows).
  2. Fair value from physics. Knowing the start price, live spot (Coinbase, cross-checked on Binance.US), seconds left and realised volatility, the chance of finishing above the start is a normal-distribution calculation — including the final-minute averaging. The council buys a side only when the book sells it well below that, after the 7%·p(1−p) taker fee.
  3. Strike-ladder arbitrage. When a 5-minute and a 15-minute window end together, Up on the lower strike is always worth at least Up on the higher strike. If prices break that by more than the fees, Up(low) + Down(high) pays $1 in every outcome — $2 if the final lands between the strikes.
  4. 25 agents, five desks. Oracle (strike, feed, cross-exchange, volatility, clock), value (model, distance, edge, Kelly, consensus), structure (ladder, complement, bounds, extremes, fees), execution (spread, depth, imbalance, quote lag, liquidity) and risk (momentum, jumps, exposure, bankroll, track record). Safety agents can veto.

Rules

Honest backtest (1,651 resolved windows, 14 hours, spot data up to a minute older than the market's): the model's probabilities were as accurate as the market's, and a model + market blend beat the market out of sample — but after fees and the spread, directional trades were only a few percent positive and not statistically proven. Ladder breaks appeared about once every 3 hours on liquid books, always paying ≥ $1. Treat directional trades as experiments; the scoreboard measures them live.

Polyscan is for study and paper trading. Minute markets are fast and competitive: bots with exchange co-location update these books in milliseconds, and a browser cannot match them. Nothing here is financial or betting advice; prediction-market trading is restricted in some jurisdictions. Trade only what you can afford to lose.
